Abstract

Pedestrians can remain relevant to navigation after leaving a robot's camera view. A visual policy therefore needs to use recent observations without treating missing or outdated information as current evidence. We present SocialSTG, a visual social-navigation framework with persistent spatio-temporal graph memory. A memory bank maintains pedestrian records and temporarily retains unmatched observations as latent human nodes. Edge-conditioned attention models their interactions with the robot and goal, while uncertainty-gated updates regulate how messages enter recurrent states associated with each record. The pooled graph representation is fused with the visual policy, and auxiliary tasks provide training signals for motion, visibility, and interaction cues. On Social-HM3D and zero-shot Social-MP3D, SocialSTG improves task completion and success-weighted efficiency over Falcon and classical planners while reducing human-collision episodes. Ablations support complementary roles for record retention, temporal gating, and graph input to the policy; motion features and graph-based auxiliary supervision mainly benefit completion and efficiency. Scene-specific adaptation in a reconstructed campus cafeteria yields further gains, with SocialSTG retaining an advantage over adapted Falcon. These gains do not extend uniformly to personal-space compliance. The results support persistent interaction memory as a useful addition to visual social navigation, while leaving the balance between goal progress and interpersonal clearance an open challenge.

SocialSTG extends a visual PointGoal policy with persistent record-indexed graph states. Retained pedestrian records remain available for up to 20 updates, while the graph branch can store up to 8 human nodes.

Method

The method separates record accessibility from recurrent interaction history. A memory bank decides which pedestrian records remain available, while record-indexed hidden states preserve learned temporal context across shifting graph slots.

01 · DYNAMIC INTERACTION GRAPH

Remember people across observations

At every step, the graph contains the robot, goal, and a bounded set of human records. YOLOv8n detections and depth-based position proxies update observed records; unmatched records become latent nodes and are removed only after the retention threshold.

02 · UNCERTAINTY-GATED MEMORY

Filter messages before recurrence

Spatial attention allocates relative importance across neighbors, while a separate sigmoid gate attenuates messages according to depth, association, occlusion, and latent-state uncertainty before they enter each node's recurrent state.

03 · GRAPH–VISUAL FUSION

Expose interaction context to the policy

Robot-query attention pools human and goal states into a graph summary. A zero-initialized residual projection adds this summary to the visual, goal, and previous-action representation before the policy LSTM.

04 · AUXILIARY SUPERVISION

Train states for social interaction

Human-node heads supervise short-horizon motion, visibility, conflict, and approach-time proxies. These training-only targets regularize the graph representation alongside PPO while padding and inactive slots remain masked.

Qualitative navigation

Detailed case evidence

The appendix records how the qualitative cases were selected, replayed, and labeled.

Selection and replay

The diagnostic collection contains 40 complete rollouts across 38 scenes, with success and failure strata covering efficient completion, contact-associated outcomes, time limits, strict-versus-reach success disagreements, and an initially unclassified failure. Four fresh multiview reruns are shown in the main figure; they were captured separately to obtain synchronized top-down and robot views.

Synchronized multiview capture

Each frame contains robot RGB, depth, a rendered top-down view, a third-person view, a navigation map, physical poses, and outcome metrics. All views are rendered from the same simulator state with action and pedestrian state held fixed. Saved frame counts are 119 and 64 for the Social-HM3D success and failure, and 105 and 141 for the cafeteria success and failure.

Observed outcomes

The Social-HM3D success passes an initial encounter and terminates at approximately 0.076 m from the goal with zero human contact. The Social-HM3D failure records a close encounter and human collision while the goal remains distant. The cafeteria success reaches approximately 0.092 m from the goal without contact; the cafeteria failure shows progress followed by a close pedestrian encounter and contact.

Additional failure modes

One episode remains stalled until the time limit with unchanged goal distance and no human collision. Another terminates prematurely while the goal is approximately 9.87 m away. These cases show that collision avoidance alone does not guarantee task completion.
